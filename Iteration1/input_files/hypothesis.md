**Title**: Bayesian Shrinkage of Factor Loadings via Hierarchical Priors for Low-Sharpe Factor Recovery

**Hypothesis**: The failure to recover low-Sharpe factors (SMB) in small-N panels is primarily driven by the high variance of OLS-estimated loadings ($\hat{\beta}$) rather than the covariance matrix ($\Sigma$) alone. I hypothesize that replacing frequentist OLS/Ledoit-Wolf estimates with a Hierarchical Bayesian model—where stock-level factor loadings are drawn from a common cross-sectional distribution (shrinkage towards the grand mean)—will significantly reduce the idiosyncratic volatility leakage observed in SMB. 

By imposing a prior on the factor loadings that reflects the known cross-sectional characteristics (e.g., size-based priors for SMB), we can stabilize the factor mimicking portfolio (FMP) weights even when $N \approx T$. This approach will outperform both standard OLS and characteristic-based sorting by effectively "denoising" the loading estimates before the portfolio construction step, allowing for statistically significant recovery of the SMB premium where previous methods failed. The success of this approach will be measured by the improvement in the Information Ratio (IR) and the attainment of $|t| > 1.96$ for the SMB factor at $N=50$, which has remained elusive in previous iterations.
