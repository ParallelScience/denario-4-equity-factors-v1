**Title**: Information Ratio Decay in Small-N Factor Mimicking Portfolios: A Signal-to-Noise Threshold Analysis

**Description**: This study quantifies the performance degradation of factor mimicking portfolios (FMPs) by comparing an "Ideal FMP," constructed using the `true_betas.csv` ground-truth loadings, against "Sample-Estimated FMPs" derived from OLS regressions on the 50-stock panel. We measure the Information Ratio (IR) decay as a function of the 3.5% monthly idiosyncratic volatility, specifically isolating the loss in premium capture for the low-Sharpe SMB factor versus the high-Sharpe WML factor. By systematically varying the cross-sectional sub-sample size ($N \in [10, 50]$), we establish the empirical threshold where the noise-to-signal ratio renders sample-estimated factor premiums statistically indistinguishable from zero. This analysis provides a rigorous benchmark for the minimum cross-sectional breadth required to reliably recover latent factor premiums in the presence of persistent idiosyncratic noise.
